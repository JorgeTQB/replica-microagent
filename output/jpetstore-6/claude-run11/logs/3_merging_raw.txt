## Analysis

Based on my investigation:

**Key Dependencies Found:**

1. **`CartActionBean`** uses `CatalogService` (from Catalog domain) to check item availability and get item details.
2. **`OrderActionBean`** uses both `AccountActionBean` and `CartActionBean` - it bridges Cart, Account, and Order.
3. **`OrderService`** uses `ItemMapper` (from Catalog domain) to update inventory quantities when orders are placed.
4. **`Order.initOrder()`** takes `Account` and `Cart` as parameters.
5. **Shopping Cart** has no service layer and no persistence - it's a session-only construct.
6. **Shopping Cart** directly depends on the **Catalog** (CatalogService) to fetch items.

**Merging Strategy (4 → 3 clusters):**

The strongest coupling exists between:
- **Shopping Cart** ↔ **Catalog**: CartActionBean uses CatalogService extensively, Cart/CartItem use Item objects
- **Shopping Cart** ↔ **Order Management**: OrderActionBean uses CartActionBean, Order.initOrder() takes Cart
- **Order Management** ↔ **Catalog**: OrderService uses ItemMapper for inventory updates

The natural merge is: **Shopping Cart + Order Management** → they share the transaction lifecycle (cart → checkout → order), and both depend on Catalog. The Shopping Cart has no independent persistence and acts as the bridge to Order Management.

This gives us exactly 3 clusters:
1. **Account Management** (unchanged)
2. **Catalog** (unchanged) 
3. **Shopping Cart & Order Management** (merged - tight coupling through the purchase flow)
