Now I have a comprehensive understanding of the application. Let me analyze the relationships and determine the best way to merge the 4 domains into 3 clusters.

**Analysis Summary:**

1. **Account Management** (4 classes) - Self-contained: Account, AccountMapper, AccountService, AccountActionBean
2. **Catalog** (8 classes) - Self-contained: Category, Product, Item, CatalogService, CategoryMapper, ProductMapper, ItemMapper, CatalogActionBean
3. **Shopping Cart** (3 classes) - Cart, CartItem, CartActionBean
4. **Order Management** (8 classes) - OrderActionBean, OrderService, OrderMapper, LineItemMapper, SequenceMapper, Order, LineItem, Sequence

**Key Relationships to Consider:**

- `CartActionBean` **uses** `CatalogService` (to check item stock and get item details)
- `OrderActionBean` **calls** both `AccountActionBean` and `CartActionBean` (session-based references)
- `Order.initOrder()` **uses** both `Account` and `Cart` objects
- `OrderService` **uses** `ItemMapper` to update inventory (cross-domain dependency with Catalog)
- Shopping Cart depends heavily on Catalog (CatalogService) for item data
- Order Management depends on Cart (to convert cart to order) and Account (for shipping info)

**Merging Decision:**

The Shopping Cart (3 classes) is tightly coupled with both Order Management and Catalog:
- Cart uses `CatalogService` for item lookup
- Orders are created from Carts (`Order.initOrder(account, cart)`)
- Cart/Order form a natural "commerce transaction" flow

The most logical merge is:
1. **Account Management** → stays as its own microservice (clear bounded context, self-contained)
2. **Catalog** → stays as its own microservice (product catalog is a core bounded context)
3. **Shopping Cart + Order Management** → merge into a single "Commerce/Order Processing" microservice

Rationale for merging Shopping Cart with Order Management:
- `OrderActionBean` directly references `CartActionBean` from the session to get cart data when creating an order
- `Order.initOrder()` takes both an Account and Cart directly
- The cart-to-order transition is a tightly coupled workflow
- Shopping Cart has no persistence layer of its own (session-only), making it dependent on Order Management to commit
- Together they represent the full purchase transaction lifecycle
