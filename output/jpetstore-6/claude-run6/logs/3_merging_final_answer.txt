Based on my thorough analysis of the codebase, I can now make my merging decision. Here's my reasoning:

**Analysis:**

1. **Account Management** (4 classes) - Standalone domain with authentication/user management. Can remain separate.

2. **Product Catalog** (8 classes) - Core catalog/browsing domain. Can remain separate.

3. **Shopping Cart** (3 classes) - The `CartActionBean` uses `CatalogService` to check item stock and retrieve items. The `Order.initOrder()` method directly uses both `Account` and `Cart`. The `OrderActionBean` calls `CartActionBean` directly (uses session-scoped cart bean).

4. **Order Management** (8 classes) - Tightly coupled with Shopping Cart: `OrderActionBean` directly accesses `CartActionBean`, `Order.initOrder()` uses `Cart` and `CartItem`, `OrderService` uses `ItemMapper` to update inventory.

**Merging Decision:**

Since we need exactly 3 clusters from 4 domains:
- **Shopping Cart** and **Order Management** are the most tightly coupled: `OrderActionBean` directly calls `CartActionBean`, gets the cart from session, `Order.initOrder()` takes `Cart` as parameter, and after order placement the cart is cleared by the order handler. These two form a natural "Purchase/Checkout" bounded context.
- **Account Management** remains standalone - it's the identity/authentication service.
- **Product Catalog** remains standalone - it's the read-oriented catalog service.

The Shopping Cart + Order Management merge makes the most architectural sense because:
1. `OrderActionBean` directly depends on `CartActionBean` (session coupling)
2. `Order.initOrder(Account, Cart)` - cart is the input to creating orders
3. `OrderService` uses `ItemMapper` to update inventory (already in catalog domain, but order processing owns the transaction)
4. Both share the checkout workflow
