Now I have a comprehensive understanding of the codebase. Let me analyze the dependencies and determine the best merge strategy.

**Analysis Summary:**

1. **Account Management** - Standalone user identity domain. Relatively independent.

2. **Catalog** - Product browsing domain. `CartActionBean` uses `CatalogService` to get item details when adding to cart.

3. **Shopping Cart** - Session-based cart domain. 
   - `CartActionBean` USES `CatalogService` (from Catalog domain)
   - `CartActionBean` is CALLED_BY `OrderActionBean` (from Order Management domain)
   - `Order` USES `Cart` and `CartItem`
   - `LineItem` is created from `CartItem`

4. **Order Management** - Order lifecycle domain.
   - `OrderActionBean` CALLS `CartActionBean` and `AccountActionBean`
   - `Order.initOrder()` takes `Account` and `Cart` as parameters
   - `OrderService` uses `ItemMapper` for inventory deduction

**Key Observations for Merging:**

- Shopping Cart and Order Management have **very strong coupling**: The `Order` domain directly uses `Cart` and `CartItem`, and `OrderActionBean` directly accesses `CartActionBean`. The cart-to-order conversion is a core workflow.
- `CartActionBean` uses `CatalogService` to fetch item details - this shows catalog dependency from cart.
- Account Management is fairly independent - it's referenced by Order for initialization but doesn't fundamentally belong with any other domain.

**Merge Decision:**
- **Cluster 1: Account Management** - Keep as standalone (user identity is a clear bounded context)
- **Cluster 2: Catalog** - Keep as standalone (product information backbone)  
- **Cluster 3: Shopping Cart + Order Management** - Merge these two because:
  - They share the same transactional workflow (cart → order)
  - `Order.initOrder()` directly consumes `Cart` and `CartItem`
  - `OrderActionBean` directly calls `CartActionBean`
  - `LineItem` is created from `CartItem`
  - Together they form the "Commerce/Transaction" bounded context
