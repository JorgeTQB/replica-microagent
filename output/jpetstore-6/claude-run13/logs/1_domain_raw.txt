**Analysis Summary:**

1. **Account/User Management Domain**: The application has a clear user account management capability with authentication (signon/signoff), profile management, and personal preferences. This is served by AccountService, AccountMapper, Account domain object, and AccountActionBean.

2. **Catalog Domain**: The application has a product catalog with categories, products, and items (inventory). CatalogService, CategoryMapper, ProductMapper, ItemMapper, and CatalogActionBean manage browsing, searching, and viewing catalog information. The database has category, product, item, inventory, and supplier tables.

3. **Shopping Cart Domain**: The Cart domain manages the shopping experience - adding items, removing items, updating quantities. CartActionBean and Cart/CartItem domain objects handle this. This is a distinct business capability that bridges catalog browsing and order placement.

4. **Order Management Domain**: Order processing is a distinct business capability covering order creation, tracking, fulfillment. OrderService, OrderMapper, LineItemMapper, and OrderActionBean manage order lifecycle. The database has orders, orderstatus, lineitem tables.

The Cart functionality could potentially be part of the Order domain (as it feeds into orders), but it has its own dedicated ActionBean and distinct Cart/CartItem domain objects. However, since Cart is essentially a pre-order state and deeply tied to the checkout process, and noting the workflow says domains should be ≥3, I'll carefully consider whether Cart merits its own domain.

The Cart domain is distinct - it has dedicated classes (Cart, CartItem, CartActionBean) with separate business logic around managing items in a session-based cart before order placement, while Order domain handles the actual order transaction and persistence. These represent different bounded contexts with different lifecycles (session vs. persistent).
